## Background: the BBM technique

A chain of inverters is the usual way to build a small delay, but every extra inverter costs roughly the dynamic energy needed to switch its input, so long delays become power-hungry, and each stage also draws direct-path (short-circuit) current while its PMOS and NMOS conduct together.

Break-before-make switching drives the PMOS and NMOS gates of an inverter with time-skewed signals. For a short *isolation time* both transistors are off and the output floats, holding its logic value on the load capacitance, so no direct path to the supply exists. The paper builds the skew with a small bidirectional delay element between the PMOS and NMOS drains of the first inverter. The element yields two outputs, A (the PMOS gate signal) and B (the NMOS gate signal). A split-input inverter then merges them again. The paper's best full-swing version, **PtNf**, uses a PMOS delay transistor in triode (Pt) plus an NMOS level-restoration transistor (Nf) whose gate is driven from the next-stage output.

The paper reports (TSMC 0.18 um, HSPICE, its own optimiser):

- PtNf needs 90 % of the standard cell's charge at T_p = 0.5 ns and 64 % at 1.0 ns, and just over 40 % less charge than the standard cell from 3.5 ns upwards;
- no benefit below about 0.35 ns;
- up to 50 % lower total switching energy in a leading-edge pulse generator.

## What I simulated

- **Circuits.** Paper Fig. 9(a), the conventional cell with four inverters, and Fig. 9(b), the BBM cell with the PtNf delay element of Fig. 8 (P1, Pt, N1, Nf, then the P2/N2 merge stage). Both have an input and an output unit inverter. Transistor counts: 8 (conventional) and 10 (BBM).
- **Analysis.** Transient, V<sub>DD</sub> = 1.8 V, 27 &deg;C, C<sub>L</sub> = 2 fF, 50 MHz input, 50 ps edges. Delay is measured 50 % to 50 % from input to output. Charge and power come from integrating the supply current over four steady-state cycles. PDP = P<sub>avg</sub> x T<sub>p</sub>. A separate Python re-integration of the raw waveforms agrees with the ngspice `.meas` values to better than 0.01 %.
- **Fair comparison.** Each topology is re-sized for the same delay target (0.5 ns and 1.0 ns) with a differential-evolution optimiser (960 simulations per case). The constraints are: delay within +/-2 %, rise/fall symmetry, full output swing, BBM level restoration, and a positive isolation time.
- **Extra studies.** Load sweep (0.5 to 10 fF), input edge-rate sweep (20 to 500 ps), one-parameter scaling sweeps from the paper's 750 ps sizing, and a delay-element-removal ablation.

## Key results

All values come from ngspice runs; see `results/summary*.json`.

| Case | Conventional | BBM (PtNf) | Saving |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 750 ps sizing, no re-sizing (delays **not** matched: 507 vs 563 ps) | Q = 67.3 fC | Q = 40.3 fC | not a fair figure |
| Re-optimised, T_p of about 0.5 ns (495 vs 505 ps) | Q = 44.1 fC, PDP = 1.96 fJ | Q = 38.3 fC, PDP = 1.74 fJ | Q 13.1 %, PDP 11.4 % |
| Re-optimised, T_p of about 1.0 ns (1016 vs 1006 ps) | Q = 64.8 fC, PDP = 5.93 fJ | Q = 49.8 fC, PDP = 4.51 fJ | Q 23.2 %, PDP 23.9 % |

Performance table for the 1.0 ns delay-matched pair (the experiment's headline comparison):

| Parameter | Conventional (re-sized) | BBM as built from the paper's 750 ps sizing | BBM (re-sized) |
|---|---|---|---|
| V<sub>DD</sub> | 1.8 V | 1.8 V | 1.8 V |
| Transistor count | 8 | 10 | 10 |
| Average power at 50 MHz | 5.84 uW | 3.63 uW | 4.48 uW |
| Delay | 1016 ps | 563 ps | 1006 ps |
| PDP | 5.93 fJ | 2.04 fJ | 4.51 fJ |
| Gate-area proxy (sum of W x L) | 2.40 um^2 | 1.00 um^2 | 1.34 um^2 |
| Charge per cycle | 64.8 fC | 40.3 fC | 49.8 fC |

The middle column is shown for completeness only: its delay does not match the reference, so it must not be quoted as an improvement.

**How this compares with the paper.** The trend is reproduced: the benefit grows with delay and is small near 0.5 ns. The magnitudes are not: my saving at 1 ns (23 %) is clearly below the paper's 36 %, and absolute charges cannot be compared because the model, the unit inverters and the load all differ.

**Observations from my own waveforms.**

- With the paper's 750 ps sizing, the isolation edge arrives 155 to 258 ps before the information edge, and the two conducting windows of the merge stage never overlap.
- The explicit crowbar charge of the conventional stage 3 is small under this model (about 0.7 fC per cycle by a min(i<sub>P</sub>, i<sub>N</sub>) estimate, out of 15.4 fC drawn by that stage). I therefore cannot attribute the saving mainly to eliminated short-circuit current. A good part probably comes from BBM letting P2 and N2 stay small.
- Node A overshoots to 2.04 V through Miller coupling, which is exactly what the paper's over/undershoot constraints are meant to suppress.
- Removing the delay element changes the delay by about a factor of four (563 ps to 155 ps), so it is not a clean mechanism test. I document it as such.
- With sizes fixed at the paper's 750 ps design, the charge saving shrinks as the load grows (41 % at 0.5 fF, 34 % at 10 fF) and with slow input edges (38.7 % at 500 ps).

## Case study summary

The case study covers the TI **SN74AUP1G04**, a single inverter gate from the Advanced Ultra-low-Power family. Datasheet values are tagged `[DS]` and my own explanations and calculations are tagged `[Expl]`. It covers:

- function and internal logic (a generic static CMOS inverter is shown, since TI does not publish the netlist);
- the 0.8 V to 3.6 V supply range and input/output levels;
- static, dynamic and capacitance specifications (I<sub>CC</sub> <= 0.9 uA, C<sub>pd</sub> = 3.9 pF typical, I<sub>off</sub> partial-power-down protection);
- propagation delay versus load and supply voltage;
- a worked dynamic-versus-static power calculation: P<sub>dyn</sub> = (C<sub>pd</sub> + C<sub>L</sub>) V<sub>CC</sub><sup>2</sup> f is about 288 uW at 1.8 V, 10 MHz and 5 pF, against at most 1.6 uW static;
- applications, trade-offs against the SN74LVC1G04, and ten prepared audience questions and answers, including how the device relates to break-before-make.

`Case_Study_Presentation.pptx` is the 6-slide, 5-minute talk for it, with speaker notes and timings.

## Reproducing everything

### 1. Simulations (ngspice)

```bash
sudo apt-get install -y ngspice          # tested with ngspice-42 on Ubuntu 24.04
pip install numpy scipy matplotlib python-docx python-pptx pillow
cd spice
# single runs; the .meas results are printed in the log
ngspice -b -o ../results/conventional_inverter.log conventional_inverter.sp
ngspice -b -o ../results/bbm_inverter.log          bbm_inverter.sp
python3 run_all.py            # baseline, ablation, scaling, C_L and edge sweeps -> ../results/summary.json (about 1-2 min)
python3 optimize.py 0.5 1.0   # differential-evolution re-sizing at T_p targets (about 10 min per target) -> ../results/opt_*.json
python3 validate_opt.py       # re-run optimised sizes on the full testbench -> ../results/summary_opt.json
python3 make_schematics.py && python3 make_plots.py
```

`optimize.py` uses a fixed seed, but results can differ slightly across SciPy and ngspice versions.

### 2. DOCX and PPTX documents

```bash
cd docs_src
python3 build_pbl.py && python3 build_cbs.py && python3 build_case.py && python3 build_pptx.py
```

The scripts read `results/*.json` and `figures/`, so every number in the reports comes straight from the simulation output.

## Assumptions and known limitations

Please read these before relying on any number in this repository.

1. **Substitute device model.** The paper's TSMC 0.18 um HSPICE model is proprietary, so I did not use it. I wrote an ngspice Level-1 model from the parameters printed in the caption of the paper's Fig. 4 (V<sub>T</sub> of +/-0.5 V, K<sub>P</sub> of 350 and 90 uA/V^2, C<sub>ox</sub> = 8.6 fF/um^2). The remaining parameters (lambda, gamma, phi, junction and overlap capacitances) are my own assumptions. Level 1 has no velocity saturation or sub-threshold leakage, so static power is zero, and I simulated 27 &deg;C only.
2. **Assumed sizes and load.** The unit-inverter sizes (860/220 nm, L = 180 nm) and C<sub>L</sub> = 2 fF are my assumptions, because the paper does not give them. The paper's Fig. 11 sizing (its T_p = 750 ps solution) is the starting point.
3. **Reconstructed circuit.** I rebuilt the circuit from the figures in the paper PDF, since no netlist was published. My reading of Nf's gate connection to the merged output follows the paper's text on feedback level restoration.
4. **Meaning of PDP.** I define PDP as P<sub>avg</sub> x T<sub>p</sub> at 50 MHz, as the assignment asks. Because P<sub>avg</sub> scales with frequency, the frequency-independent charge or energy per cycle is the cleaner metric. Area is only a sum-of-W x L gate-area proxy; I did no layout.
5. **Small optimiser.** My optimiser is far smaller than the paper's 100-node SADE run, so my sizings are not guaranteed global optima. I optimised only at 0.5 and 1.0 ns, and did not re-test the short-delay region (0.35 ns and below) where the paper finds no advantage.
6. **No clean mechanism test.** The crowbar-charge estimate is small and the delay-element-removal ablation changes the delay by about 4x, so my data cannot isolate how much of the saving comes from eliminated short-circuit current and how much from smaller devices.
7. **Placeholders.** Name, roll number, batch, dates, signatures and marks are untouched placeholders in the committed files.
8. **Case-study data.** Values were checked against TI datasheet SCES571K (Rev. K, June 2014), retrieved on 20 Oct 2026. Two points are worth re-checking against the PDF: (a) the I<sub>CC</sub>, delta I<sub>CC</sub>, I<sub>off</sub> and I<sub>I</sub> rows showed only two numbers in text extraction, which I read as (25 &deg;C max, -40 to 85 &deg;C max); (b) TI's product-page parametric table shows a maximum supply current of 10 uA, which disagrees with the datasheet's 0.9 uA. I used the datasheet value and noted the discrepancy in the report. Statements about similar devices (SN74LVC1G04, SN74UVP1G04) come from TI's product page and are not independently verified.
9. **Third-party figures.** Paper Figs. 1, 8 (PtNf) and 9 appear in the PBL review as cropped images under the paper's CC BY licence, with attribution. All other diagrams are redrawn by me.
